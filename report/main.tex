\begin{abstract}
Audio-visual emotion recognition (AV-MER) on the CREMA-D dataset has attracted
significant attention, yet reported accuracies vary wildly---from 69\% to
95\%---due to inconsistent evaluation protocols, particularly random clip splits
that leak actor identity into test sets.
We present \textbf{PAM-VLM} (Perception-Aligned Multimodal VLM), a three-tier
architecture that addresses both the methodological and modeling shortcomings of
prior work.
First, we exploit CREMA-D's unique per-modality vote annotations
(voice-only, face-only, and audio-visual conditions) to train each encoder branch
with \emph{soft labels} derived from human vote distributions rather than hard
one-hot labels, improving calibration under label noise.
Second, a bidirectional cross-attention fusion module produces a deterministic
six-class classifier as the primary metric source.
Third, an optional Qwen2.5-0.5B LLM with LoRA acts as a reasoning reconciler for
high audio-video disagreement cases and enables prompt-based zero-shot transfer
to unseen tasks.
All experiments use a strict actor-independent split (70/10/11 actors) with
Macro-F1 and UAR as primary metrics.
Training is currently in progress on an A100 GPU cluster; this report presents
the design, dataset analysis, and experimental protocol.
\end{abstract}

emotion recognition, multimodal learning, label distribution learning,

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Automatic emotion recognition from speech and facial expressions is a core
capability for human-computer interaction, mental health monitoring, and
affective computing.
Multimodal systems that jointly process audio and video consistently outperform
unimodal counterparts~\cite{cao2014crema}, yet building a reliable, reproducible
system remains challenging due to label ambiguity and inconsistent benchmarking
practices.

The CREMA-D dataset~\cite{cao2014crema} is a widely used benchmark offering
7,442 clips from 91 actors spanning six canonical emotions.
Crucially, it provides crowd-sourced votes under three distinct perceptual
conditions---voice-only, face-only, and audio-visual---a resource that has been
largely ignored by prior work, which typically collapses votes into a single hard
label.

A survey of published results reveals a fundamental comparability problem:
reported accuracies on CREMA-D range from~69\% to~95\% (see Table~\ref{tab:benchmark}).
We attribute this variance not to model capability but to evaluation protocol,
specifically random clip splits that allow the same actor to appear in both
training and test sets.
When the same voice and face are seen at train time, a model can exploit speaker
identity as a shortcut rather than learning genuine emotion representations---a
phenomenon we term \emph{actor leakage}.

The contributions of this work are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Benchmark critique with evidence:} we systematically show that
    high-accuracy CREMA-D results correlate with random splits and
    augmentation-before-split pipelines, and establish an honest baseline range
    of 70--85\% under actor-independent evaluation.
  \item \textbf{Per-modality soft-label supervision:} we leverage CREMA-D's
    three vote conditions to construct per-clip label distributions for each
    modality, applying Label Distribution Learning~\cite{geng2016ldl} to improve
    calibration and Macro-F1 under noisy labels.
  \item \textbf{VLM reconciler with zero-shot transfer:} an optional
    Qwen2.5-0.5B~\cite{qwen2025} branch with LoRA~\cite{hu2022lora} provides
    explainable reconciliation of audio-video conflicts and enables
    prompt-only zero-shot transfer to held-out tasks, a capability absent in
    deterministic classifiers.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Audio-Visual Emotion Recognition}

Cross-attention fusion between audio and visual streams has emerged as the
dominant paradigm for AV-MER.
AVT-CA~\cite{avtca2024} applies bidirectional audio-video transformer fusion
across multiple benchmarks including CREMA-D.
Nature hybrid fusion~\cite{naturefusion2026} specifically evaluates under
speaker-independent conditions, reporting results in the 80\%+ range---the
honest upper bound we target.

\subsection{Benchmark Protocol Critique}
\label{ssec:critique}

Table~\ref{tab:benchmark} summarizes published results alongside the evaluation
protocol where known.
Entries achieving $\geq$89\% share a common pattern: random clip splits, and in
several cases, data augmentation applied \emph{before} the train/test split.
The most telling indicator of leakage is when a model surpasses the human
agreement ceiling: crowd-sourced annotators on CREMA-D achieve only 60--75\%
pairwise agreement even when provided both audio and visual modalities.
A machine exceeding this ceiling on the same data is almost certainly exploiting
identity rather than affective content.

\subsection{Label Distribution Learning}

Geng~\cite{geng2016ldl} formalizes learning from label distributions rather than
crisp labels, showing consistent gains on datasets with inherent ambiguity.
CREMA-D's crowd-sourced vote structure is a natural fit: rather than discarding
annotator disagreement, we treat it as a calibration signal.

\subsection{VLMs for Affective Computing}

Vision-language models bring world knowledge about emotional expression but
perform poorly as zero-shot emotion classifiers (40--55\% reported on
face-based tasks).
Their strength lies in compositional reasoning over conflicting modality signals
and in prompt-driven transfer to unseen label sets---exactly the roles we assign
to our optional VLM branch.

\section{Proposed Method: PAM-VLM}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Architecture Overview}

PAM-VLM comprises three tiers (Figure~\ref{fig:arch}):
(1)~modality-specific encoders producing temporal token sequences;
(2)~bidirectional cross-attention fusion culminating in a deterministic
six-class classifier;
(3)~an optional LLM reconciler that operates on fusion embeddings.

\subsection{Encoders}

\textbf{Audio encoder.}
Raw waveforms at 16\,kHz are processed by
WavLM-base-plus~\cite{chen2022wavlm} ($\sim$94M parameters, frozen),
producing hidden states of dimension 768.
A linear projector maps these to the shared dimension $d=256$.

\textbf{Visual encoder.}
For each sampled frame, EfficientNet-B0~\cite{tan2019efficientnet}
($\sim$5.3M parameters, frozen) extracts a 1280-dim feature vector.
A two-layer Transformer encoder ($d=256$, 4 heads) models temporal
dependencies across the $F=16$ sampled frames, yielding visual tokens
$\mathbf{V}\in\mathbb{R}^{B\times F\times 256}$.
Face detection and alignment are performed offline using an OpenCV Haar
cascade; detection failures fall back to center crop (counted separately).

\subsection{Bidirectional Cross-Attention Fusion}

Audio tokens $\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{B\times T_a\times 256}$ and visual
tokens $\mathbf{V}$ are fused via two parallel attention operations:
audio queries attend to video, and video queries attend to audio.
Residual connections and layer normalization follow each operation.
Gated pooling then collapses the temporal dimensions:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{g} = \sigma\!\left(\mathbf{W}_g[\bar{\mathbf{A}};\bar{\mathbf{V}}]\right),
  \quad
  \mathbf{e} = \mathbf{g}\odot\bar{\mathbf{A}} + (1-\mathbf{g})\odot\bar{\mathbf{V}},
\end{equation}
where $\bar{\cdot}$ denotes masked mean pooling over valid (non-padded) positions
and $\mathbf{e}\in\mathbb{R}^{B\times 256}$ is the fusion embedding.
Three linear heads project $\mathbf{e}$ (or the un-pooled audio/video tokens)
to logits $\hat{\mathbf{y}}_\text{audio}$, $\hat{\mathbf{y}}_\text{video}$,
and $\hat{\mathbf{y}}_\text{av}\in\mathbb{R}^{B\times 6}$.
\textbf{The fusion head $\hat{\mathbf{y}}_\text{av}$ is the sole source
of reported benchmark metrics.}

\subsection{Per-Modality Soft Labels}
\label{ssec:soft}

CREMA-D provides annotator votes under three perceptual conditions.
We aggregate votes per clip per condition into Laplace-smoothed probability
vectors:
\begin{equation}
  \tilde{p}_c^{(i)} = \frac{n_c^{(i)} + \alpha}{\sum_j n_j^{(i)} + 6\alpha},
  \quad \alpha = 1,
\end{equation}
where $n_c^{(i)}$ is the vote count for emotion $c$ under condition~$i$
(voice, face, or AV).
Coverage is 100\% across all 7,442 clips (no fallback required).

Figure~\ref{fig:softlabel} illustrates how a single clip's vote distribution
differs across the three conditions---disagreement that hard labels discard.

\begin{figure}[t]

    showing vote distributions under voice-only (condition 1),\\

  \caption{Per-modality vote distributions for one example clip.
    Hard-label supervision would collapse all three bars to the majority class,

  \label{fig:softlabel}
\end{figure}

The multi-term training objective is:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L} = 0.6\,D_\text{KL}(\hat{y}_\text{av}\!\parallel\!\tilde{p}_\text{av})
              + 0.2\,D_\text{KL}(\hat{y}_\text{audio}\!\parallel\!\tilde{p}_\text{voice})
              + 0.2\,D_\text{KL}(\hat{y}_\text{video}\!\parallel\!\tilde{p}_\text{face}),
  \label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
where each term is logged independently and controllable via ablation flags.

\subsection{VLM Reconciler (Optional)}

When audio and video predictions diverge---quantified as
$D_\text{KL}(\hat{y}_\text{audio}\parallel\hat{y}_\text{video})$ exceeding a
threshold---the fusion embedding $\mathbf{e}$ is projected into the token space
of Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct~\cite{qwen2025} via a learned linear projector.
LoRA adapters~\cite{hu2022lora} with rank~16 are applied to query and value
projections in each attention layer ($\sim$3M trainable parameters).
The LLM generates a constrained output of the form
\texttt{<emotion>angry</emotion>} plus a one-sentence rationale.
An invalid-format parser enforces label validity; the invalid-output rate is
reported alongside accuracy.

Crucially, this branch is \emph{disabled} during baseline and soft-label training,
keeping the training objective identical to prior models for fair comparison.

\section{Experimental Protocol}
\label{sec:exp}

\subsection{Dataset and Splits}

We use CREMA-D with a strict \textbf{actor-independent} split
(seed~42): 70 actors for training (5,721 clips), 10 for validation
(820 clips), and 11 for test (901 clips).
Disjointness is asserted programmatically; a SHA-256 checksum of the manifest
is stored with every checkpoint.
Data augmentation (time-shift, noise) is applied \emph{after} splitting, on the
training partition only, preventing augmentation leakage.

\subsection{Metrics}

Primary: \textbf{Macro-F1} and \textbf{UAR} (unweighted average recall
$=$ macro recall).
Secondary: accuracy/WAR, per-class P/R/F1, confusion matrix, and Expected
Calibration Error (ECE) for measuring probability calibration.
For the generative VLM branch we additionally report invalid-format rate and
average generated token count.

\subsection{Baselines and Ablations}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Audio-only:} WavLM + linear head (CE loss).
  \item \textbf{Video-only:} EfficientNet + temporal Transformer + linear head.
  \item \textbf{Fusion, hard label:} full PAM-VLM backbone with CE loss.
  \item \textbf{Fusion, soft label:} full loss in Eq.~\eqref{eq:loss};
    ablated per term.
  \item \textbf{Fusion + VLM reconciler:} adds LoRA branch.
  \item \textbf{Zero-shot cross-task:} VLM branch with modified prompt,
    no weight update, on a held-out target dataset.
\end{enumerate}

All runs share the same split, preprocessing, and training budget for fair
comparison.

\subsection{Implementation}

Audio: 16\,kHz mono, processor from \texttt{transformers}.
Video: 16 frames at 224$\times$224, OpenCV Haar face detection.
Optimizer: AdamW ($\eta=3\times10^{-4}$, weight decay $0.01$).
Effective batch size: 32 (batch~8, gradient accumulation~4), AMP enabled.
Early stopping patience: 6 epochs on validation Macro-F1.
Hardware: NVIDIA A100 (SLURM cluster).

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented PAM-VLM, an audio-visual emotion recognition system for CREMA-D
that addresses systematic benchmarking flaws in prior work and introduces
per-modality soft-label supervision drawn from the dataset's unique vote
structure.
A strict actor-independent evaluation protocol ensures that reported results
reflect genuine emotion understanding rather than speaker memorization.
The optional VLM branch provides explainability and cross-task generalization
orthogonal to classification accuracy.
Ongoing training on an A100 cluster will furnish quantitative results to
complete this study.
